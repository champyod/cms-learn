# Logo brief

Nothing is drawn until the meaning is agreed. This file is that agreement: what the mark has to say,
what it has to survive, what it must not become, and how a candidate is judged.

## What cms-learn actually is

Four facts the mark may draw on, because they are true of the product and not of the category:

1. **Progress is gated by evidence, not by time.** A task is served only when machine verification
   passed: a reference solution, a brute-force differential, measured limits. Nothing is "done"
   because someone said so.
2. **A verdict is objective.** The platform runs its own judge; a submission comes back as a verdict
   with per-subtask weights, not as an opinion.
3. **Two routes, one bank.** The USACO ladder and the IOI path are different ways through the same
   tasks, chosen by the student.
4. **Failure is part of the method.** Subtasks exist so a partial idea scores, hints unlock on
   attempts, and a decoy can be attempted before the real idea. Getting it wrong is the designed
   first step.

## Meaning territories

A mark carries **two or three** of these, layered rather than adjacent. Adjacent meanings produce a
diagram — two symbols sitting next to each other. Layered meanings produce a mark: one read wins at
small size, and the others resolve as the mark gets larger or is looked at twice.

The three layers, and the test for each:

| Layer | Where it is read | What carries it | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1 silhouette** | 16 px, one glance | the overall shape | the shape needs the other layers to be recognisable |
| **L2 structure** | 48 px and up | a gap, a break, an offset, a tick | it contradicts L1 or introduces a second subject |
| **L3 proportion** | on reflection | rhythm, weight ramp, alignment of parts | it can only be explained in words |

A layered mark is allowed to be describable three ways; it is not allowed to look like three marks.
If L1 is unclear, the candidate is dropped no matter how good L2 and L3 are.

| # | Territory | Comes from | Abstract shape it implies | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **Threshold** — passing from one state to another | draft → verified → published; unseen → solved | a frame with a gap, or two planes meeting at an opening | reads as a door or a bookmark |
| **T2** | **Registration** — exact alignment, checked truth | the judge, and verification-by-evidence | crop marks, ticks around a centre, a crosshair | reads as a printer's mark or a target |
| **T3** | **Iteration** — attempt, verdict, revise | productive failure, hints, subtasks | an open loop with an exit, or a returning stroke | reads as refresh or undo |
| **T4** | **Ascent** — increasing difficulty | difficulty bands, the ladder | weight increasing along a diagonal | stairs, and every other ladder mark |
| **T5** | **Duality** — one origin, two routes | two tracks over one bank | a single stem that divides | reads as a fork or a Y |
| **T6** | **Accumulation** — a bank that grows | generated content, an expanding corpus | nested or repeated forms at increasing scale | reads as a stack or a signal |

Two of these are unusual in this category and worth weighing for that reason alone: **T2** because
nobody brands a learning tool with precision, and **T3** because an unfinished loop says "keep going"
without an arrow.

## What the mark must survive

- **16 px.** It is a favicon and an app tile before it is anything else. Below 24 px, shape count
  decides survival, not detail.
- **One colour.** A single-colour version must be as strong as the two-tone one; it will be used
  printed, embossed and in a terminal.
- **Two grounds.** `#0f110c` dark and white. The palette is dark-first; the mark cannot depend on it.
- **No text, no gradients, no shadows, no strokes thinner than 2 units at 32.**
- **2 px radius maximum** on any corner, per the token layer.
- **Square canvas.** A favicon is square; a wide mark forces a second asset.

## What it must not become

Each of these has already been considered and dropped, and it is worth writing down so it is not
re-proposed:

- a ladder, stair, or arrow — the category default
- angle brackets, braces, `</>` — says "developer", not "learning"
- a book, graduation cap, lightbulb, rocket
- a node graph of three dots — indistinguishable from every ML logo
- a terminal prompt in a panel — a CLI tool, not a platform
- three bars of increasing height — a dashboard
- letterforms and monograms — tried, and they need the wordmark to mean anything

## How a candidate is judged

Scored 1–5 on each, and a candidate that scores 3 or less on any of the first three is dropped
regardless of total:

1. **Meaning** — does it carry the territory without a caption?
2. **Distinctiveness** — would it be confused with another mark in this space?
3. **Small-size survival** — is the silhouette readable at 16 px?
4. **Tonal fit** — does it belong beside a monospace, single-accent interface?
5. **Extendability** — does it still work as a favicon, an app tile, a single-colour stamp, and a
   large mark on a poster?

## Decided by this brief

- Abstract, no text. The wordmark is a separate asset that sits beside the mark.
- Two tones maximum: one neutral from the token layer, one accent.
- Square canvas, single-colour capable.

## Open

- Which combination of territories, from the six above.
- Settled: one idea at every size, with a simplified build at 16 px that keeps the same silhouette.

## The mark

One mark, `design/logo.svg`, on a 64-unit grid. No plate, no backdrop: the symbol is drawn on whatever
ground it sits on. Three parts, and each one exists because the meaning needs it.

### Meaning

**A bank larger than the container.** The product generates tasks on demand, so the supply is not the
shelf — it is the generator behind the shelf. The mark shows one unit that is already past the edge.

### Parts

| Part | Form | Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | a square with one edge interrupted | the bank as a container — and a container that yields rather than truncates |
| **Units** | three equal squares, equally spaced, on one line | tasks of equal standing; only the quantity is unbounded |
| **Crossing unit** | the last square, the only one in accent, passing through the gap | the next task, already existing, not yet seen |

### Why each thing is where it is

- **The units are equal in size** — a bank where the generated task is worth less than a curated one
  would be a different product. Equal size says equal standing.
- **The spacing is equal** — 20, 35, 50 on the centre line. A tighter or looser third gap would mean
  the supply is uneven, which it is not: every request draws from the same generator.
- **The gap is exactly as tall as the crossing unit plus one unit of air** — the frame is not broken
  decoratively; it opens only enough for the unit to pass, which is what makes the break a reason
  rather than a style.
- **Only the crossing unit carries the accent** — it is the one that represents what the student has
  not seen yet, and the only element whose position is surprising.
- **There is no fourth unit** — the mark shows a supply continuing, not a supply enumerated. A fourth
  would make it a queue; three crossing an edge makes it endless.

### At each size

- **16 px** — the frame reads as a container and the lit square as the exception. That is the whole
  mark: a box, and something leaving it.
- **48 px** — the equal spacing and the exact gap width become visible.
- **Single colour** — the frame and the two inner units share one tone, the crossing unit the second.
  The meaning survives with two tones, and with one the crossing unit is simply the lightest.

### Simplified build

At 16 px the middle unit is dropped, leaving the frame and two units. The silhouette does not change:
a container, and a unit past its edge.
